Manual K-Means Simulation

A step-by-step manual simulation of the K-Means algorithm: 100 records, 3 initial centroids, and 3 full iterations of distance calculation, cluster assignment, and centroid update.

Concepts Manual Exercise Application

Dataset context

Mall Customer Segmentation dataset (Badrnezhad, H., 2026, Kaggle) — 100 store customers identified by a CustomerID (1000–1099). For each customer, the annual income and the spending score assigned by the store are recorded. The goal is to segment customers into groups with similar purchasing behavior.

Variable X — Annual Income (k$)

Annual income of the customer, in thousands of dollars. Continuous numerical variable indicating purchasing power.

Variable Y — Spending Score (1–100)

Spending score assigned by the store; higher values mean the customer spends more relative to their income.

Final Interpretation of the Manual Exercise

What each cluster represents

Cluster 1 · 25 customers

Centroid ≈ (24.4, 63.9). Low-income customers (X between 15 and 39.7) with a relatively high spending score (average Y of 64). They spend a lot relative to what they earn.

Cluster 2 · 56 customers

Centroid ≈ (45.3, 50.2). Medium income and medium spending. It is the largest group and represents the average customer of the store.

Cluster 3 · 19 customers

Centroid ≈ (82.0, 33.5). High-income customers (X from 69.3) with low to moderate spending (average Y of 33). They have purchasing power that is not being used today.

What it means to be together

Two customers in the same cluster have a similar income level and spending behavior, so the store could treat them with the same strategy: promotions and affordable prices for Cluster 1, standard products for Cluster 2, and higher-value offers or loyalty programs for Cluster 3.

Centroid locations

C1 moved from (15, 78) to (24.44, 63.88), C2 from (51.3, 59) to (45.32, 50.16), and C3 from (89.1, 3) to (82.03, 33.47). All three moved toward the center of their groups; C3 moved the most because it started in an extreme corner (the customer with the lowest spending).

How the clustering changed

Cluster sizes: Iteration 1 = 18 / 71 / 11; Iteration 2 = 24 / 58 / 18; Iteration 3 = 25 / 56 / 19. The central cluster gradually gave customers to the two extremes as the centroids moved closer to their groups. The variance dropped from 629.0 (ungrouped) to 236.2, then to 169.1, and finally to 167.2, and in iteration 3 only 2 records changed cluster: the clustering is nearly stable, although it has not fully converged yet.
