Clustering Application

Apply the K-Means clustering algorithm to segment mall customers based on their annual income and spending score.

Problem Context

Customer segmentation is a common application of unsupervised learning. By analyzing customer characteristics, businesses can identify distinct groups with similar behaviors and tailor their strategies accordingly.

In this application, the K-Means algorithm is used to group mall customers based on two numerical variables: their annual income and their spending score.

Purpose of the Application

The application groups customers into clusters and identifies the characteristics of each segment to support business decision-making.

What problem is being analyzed?

The problem consists of grouping customers into segments according to their income and spending behavior.

What do the data represent?

Each record represents a mall customer with their annual income and spending score.

What are the input variables?

The input variables are Annual Income (k$) and Spending Score (1-100).

What does the model identify?

The model identifies groups of customers with similar income and spending patterns.

What are the possible clusters?

The model works with 5 clusters, each representing a distinct customer segment.

What does the model discover?

The K-Means algorithm discovers natural groupings in the data without predefined labels.

Dataset Justification

The Mall Customer Segmentation Dataset was selected because it provides realistic customer data suitable for clustering analysis.

Why this dataset?
  • Contains 1,000 customer records, meeting the minimum requirement.
  • Includes demographic and behavioral variables suitable for segmentation.
  • Widely used in educational and business contexts.
Why these variables?
  • Annual Income (k$) reflects purchasing power.
  • Spending Score (1-100) reflects shopping behavior.
  • Together, they reveal clear customer segments.

Model Information

The application uses the K-Means clustering algorithm with 5 clusters to segment customers based on two numerical variables.

Input Variables (X)
  • Annual Income (k$): customer's annual income in thousands of dollars.
  • Spending Score (1-100): score assigned by the mall based on customer behavior.
Number of Clusters (K)

The algorithm groups the data into 5 clusters.

Cluster 0 Cluster 1 Cluster 2 Cluster 3 Cluster 4
K-Means Configuration

The algorithm uses Euclidean distance to assign each customer to the nearest centroid.

The centroids are recalculated iteratively until convergence.
